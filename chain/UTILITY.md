# What Is the Mandala FOR? — the utility the whole wheel hangs on

Curtis's flywheel is built. The vault is built. The chain is built. But a
wheel with nothing to turn is decoration. A token nobody needs is a
collectible, not a currency. This paper lays out four honest candidates
for the mandala's utility — drawn from his world, not from hype — and ends
with a recommendation. **The decision is his.**

---

## Candidate 1 — Constellation commerce: the mandala as money for his stores

**The idea.** He already ordered it: stores for Valhalla and throughout the
constellation where the mandala can be used. The mandala becomes the way
people pay inside his world — for builds, lessons, art, box sessions,
whatever his stores sell.

**What it demands technically.** Priced goods, a checkout that moves
mandalas holder-to-store (the contract's `transfer` already does this),
receipts on the chain, and refunds that work. The stores are the biggest
build of the four.

**What makes it REAL vs hype.** Real: every mandala spent buys something
somebody wanted, which is the oldest definition of money. The flywheel's
fee line finally has something to feed on — real volume, not wash trading.
Hype: stores with nothing worth buying, prices nobody honors, or mandalas
accepted "for exposure."

**Failure mode if weak.** An empty mall. If the shelves are bare, the
currency is a souvenir. Commerce utility lives or dies on *inventory* —
things worth the mandalas.

**The hard question only Curtis can answer.** *What will the first ten
things for sale be — and who made them?*

---

## Candidate 2 — Access: the mandala as the key to experiences

**The idea.** Hold (or spend) mandalas to unlock things: Pandora's Box
sessions, premium voice lessons, early builds, a seat at the table. The
token is a ticket, not just a coin.

**What it demands technically.** A gate that checks a holder's balance
(the contract's `balances_of` already answers), tiers (e.g., 100 mandalas
opens the door, 1,000 opens the inner room), and expiry or one-time-use
logic so a ticket can't be reused forever.

**What makes it REAL vs hype.** Real: the experience behind the door is
worth the key — people pay for the box session, the lesson, the build, and
the mandala is just how they pay. Hype: the door opens onto nothing, or
the same thing is free next door without the token.

**Failure mode if weak.** A velvet rope around an empty room. Access
utility is only as strong as the thing accessed. It also concentrates
power: whoever holds the most mandalas gets the most access, which needs
watching.

**The hard question only Curtis can answer.** *What experience do you have
today that someone would trade real effort to get into?*

---

## Candidate 3 — Community fuel: tipping and rewarding the helpers

**The idea.** Mandalas flow person to person: tip the one who taught you,
reward the one who kept the watch, fund the builder's next project. The
token becomes the community's thank-you, with weight.

**What it demands technically.** Almost nothing new — `transfer` is the
whole machine. What it needs is *culture*: visible tipping, leaderboards
of givers (not just holders), and maybe a community chest that the Safety
Committee oversees.

**What makes it REAL vs hype.** Real: gratitude with a ledger behind it.
People already tip with words; the mandala makes the thanks countable and
the helpers visible. It is the cheapest utility to start and the hardest
to fake — you cannot wash-trade genuine thanks for long. Hype: insiders
tipping insiders in a circle, manufacturing "activity" that means nothing.

**Failure mode if weak.** A circle of back-patting. If the same five
wallets tip each other, it is theater. It needs *new* people earning
mandalas for *new* help, constantly.

**The hard question only Curtis can answer.** *Who helped you this month
that deserved more than words — and would mandalas have said it better?*

---

## Candidate 4 — Governance weight: a voice in community decisions

**The idea.** Mandalas buy a say: input to the Safety Committee, votes on
what gets built next, a voice in the constellation's direction. Stake, not
just spend.

**What it demands technically.** Vote counting weighted by holdings or by
locked holdings (the contract's `lock` fits here — locked mandalas as
committed votes), proposal records on the chain, and protection against
one big wallet buying the whole election.

**What makes it REAL vs hype.** Real: decisions get better when the people
affected have a weighted voice, and locking for votes means voters have
skin in the game. Hype: plutocracy with nicer branding — the richest
wallet wins every vote and everyone knows it.

**Failure mode if weak.** The whale problem. Without caps or quadratic
weighting, governance tokens become "whoever has the most decides," which
is the opposite of his "all for one and one for all."

**The hard question only Curtis can answer.** *What decision would you
trust the community to make — and what decision would you never put to a vote?*

---

## The recommendation (not a decision)

**Start with Candidate 1 — constellation commerce — with Candidate 3
(community fuel) running beside it from day one.**

Why commerce first: he already ordered the stores, so the build has a
head start; it gives the flywheel real volume to feed on, which is the
one thing models can't fake; and "I bought it with mandalas" is the
sentence that makes a currency real in a person's mind.

Why fuel beside it: tipping costs almost nothing to start, it seeds
mandalas into new hands (solving commerce's cold-start problem — shoppers
need mandalas before they can spend them), and it grows the culture the
stores will need.

Why not access or governance yet: access needs experiences worth gating
(build those first, gate them later), and governance needs a community
large enough that voting means something (earn the voters first).

The order, then: **fuel the people → stock the shelves → open the doors →
share the votes.** Each step earns the next one.

But Curtis decides. This paper recommends; he rules.
